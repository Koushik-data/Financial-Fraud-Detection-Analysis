\# 📊 Key Business Insights



\- Analyzed \*\*50,000+ banking transactions\*\* using PostgreSQL and Python to identify fraud patterns, transaction anomalies, and operational risk indicators.



\- Identified \*\*16,067 fraudulent transactions\*\*, resulting in an overall fraud rate of \*\*32.13%\*\* across the transaction dataset.



\- Total fraudulent transaction exposure reached approximately \*\*₹1.60 Million\*\*, highlighting significant financial risk within digital transaction systems.



\- \*\*ATM Withdrawals\*\* recorded the highest fraud rate at \*\*32.49%\*\*, followed closely by Online Transactions and Bank Transfers.



\- Fraud activity showed strong geographic concentration, with \*\*Tokyo (20.63%)\*\*, \*\*New York (20.05%)\*\*, and \*\*Sydney (19.88%)\*\* contributing the highest share of fraud cases.



\- Peak fraud activity occurred during \*\*11 AM\*\*, recording over \*\*700+ fraudulent transactions\*\*, while nearly \*\*24% of fraud cases\*\* occurred during late-night operational hours.



\- Transaction velocity analysis revealed that users performing unusually high transaction frequencies within short time periods demonstrated elevated fraud probability.



\- High-value transactions showed significantly greater fraud exposure, indicating fraudsters primarily target larger financial transactions.



\- Fraud patterns followed a Pareto-style distribution, where a limited number of users and locations contributed disproportionately to total fraud activity.
